# Suction grasp pipeline: plan

> The plan sections below are the original design. Several values changed during the work (gripper model, ray counts, safety factor, pre-grasp distance, time budget, project layout); the progress log at the end records each change, and [README.md](README.md) has the current values. Files under `db/` and `logs/` mentioned in the log are generated by the scripts and are not in the repository.

This is a standalone pipeline that finds suction grasps for the G2's 4-cup head. It works in two phases:

| Phase | When | Input | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Grasp database | Offline, once per part | Gripper model + part CAD mesh | Ranked, validated grasps in the **part frame** |
| 2. Grasp selection | Execution time, per pick | Part ID, part pose in the **robot frame**, scene (support plane, obstacles), current joints | Ranked grasps in the robot frame, each with a collision-free G2 right-arm IK posture |

Decisions (2026-10-22):
- standalone project (`suction_grasp_pipeline/`), which copies what it needs from `g2_pybullet_sim`;
- current 4-cup head only;
- phase 2 returns ranked grasps plus a reachability check;
- parts may rest in any stable pose.

The only file that depends on `g2_pybullet_sim` is the simulation validation script (step 6).

---

## Frames and conventions

- **Part frame**: the CAD mesh's own frame, with no re-centring. The part pose given at execution time is defined on the same mesh. On the real robot that pose comes from the pose-estimation service, so the database must use the same model files and the same frame as that service. This needs checking (see open items).
- **Tool frame** (`suction_center`):
  - origin at the centre of the cup square, in the plane of the cup tips;
  - +z is the approach direction, pointing into the part;
  - +x and +y lie along the sides of the cup square.
- **Flange pose** = tool pose · `T_tool_flange`. This transform is fixed and read from the gripper URDF.
- **Grasp** = a tool pose in the part frame (a 4×4 matrix stored as position + quaternion), plus its scores.
- **Symmetry**: the cup square looks the same after a 90° turn about the tool z axis, so the seal result repeats every 90°. The head does not: it has a one-sided lobe. *(Corrected in step 3.)* The database therefore samples and collision-checks turns over the full 360°, and phase 2 uses each grasp as stored.
- Units: metres and radians everywhere. CAD in millimetres is converted on load.

## Phase 1: grasp database (offline)

Script: `build_grasp_db.py --part <mesh> --gripper gripper.yaml`. Output: `db/<part>.npz`, `db/<part>.json` and `db/<part>_grasps.png`.

### Inputs

- **Part mesh**: PLY, OBJ or STL in the CAD frame.
  - The JT files need conversion first. The earlier PLY conversions were in a scratchpad that has since been deleted; the pose-estimation project holds converted models.
  - The sheet-metal parts are thin shells, so both sides of the sheet are graspable surfaces.
- **`gripper.yaml`**:
  - cup tip positions in the tool frame: a 50 mm square (cup centres 50 mm apart, measured), 4 cups;
  - cup lip radius: 12 mm (24 mm custom 1.5-bellows cups, the cup data sheet);
  - bellows compliance (how far the rim can deviate from a plane and still seal);
  - `MIN_CUPS` = 3;
  - vacuum pressure (75 kPa, the EVS08 maximum) and safety factor (1.5 g), set in `gripper.json`;
  - `T_tool_flange`;
  - collision model of the suction head: the JODELL EVS08 body (the EVS08 data sheet) and cups, built into the copied URDF by `tools/copy_robot.py`.

### Steps

1. **Load and clean.** Convert units, merge duplicate vertices and fix face winding. Compute the centre of mass: area-weighted for a thin sheet, volume-weighted for a closed solid. Compute the mass from area × sheet thickness × density, as `build_parts.py` does.
2. **Stable poses.** Run `trimesh.poses.compute_stable_poses` and keep the poses with probability > 1% (at most about 6). For each one, store the rotation from the part frame to "resting on a plane". Step 7 uses these to tag which grasps are usable in which resting pose.
3. **Sample candidates (Dex-Net 3.0 style).**
   - Draw about 20 k surface points, area-weighted, on both sides of the sheet.
   - At each point the approach direction is minus the surface normal. A side is kept only if a short ray outward from the point is free, which drops the hidden side of a fold.
   - Each point gets 24 turns about the approach axis: 0–345° in 15° steps, because the head is not symmetric. *(Step 3: first 6 turns over 0–75°, which made mirrored twin parts disagree.)*
   - This gives about 120 k candidates. Cheap filters (steps 4 and 5) remove most of them before the expensive checks.
4. **Seal check, per cup.** Ray-cast along the approach axis at the cup centre and at 8 points on the cup rim. A cup seals if:
   - all 9 rays hit the part (no hole or edge under the rim);
   - the rim hits lie within `SEAL_TOL` of a plane (bellows compliance, placeholder 1.5 mm);
   - the surface normal at the centre is within 30° of the cup axis.

   A grasp is valid if at least `MIN_CUPS` cups seal. This is stricter than the simulation's single-ray rule. Step 7 checks both rules.
5. **Collision check.** At the grasp pose, the suction head and wrist meshes must not intersect the part, apart from the cup lips. The same must hold along the 100 mm straight approach, checked in 10 mm steps.
   - PyBullet does this, as the current evaluator does: the part is loaded as a fine convex decomposition (`build_parts.py`'s k-means pieces) and `getClosestPoints` is called.
   - Only the gripper head is loaded, not the whole robot.
6. **Hold check (Dex-Net 3.0 wrench resistance, simplified).**
   - Each sealed cup can pull with at most vacuum × cup area, and can slide with at most μ × that force.
   - The check is that the sealed cups resist the part's weight × safety factor (2 g, to cover the carry) acting at the centre of mass, for the gripper pointing straight down.
   - The result is a margin from 0 to 1. Distance from the centre of mass mostly matters through the peel moment.
7. **Robustness (Dex-Net-style perturbation).**
   - Re-run steps 4 and 5 on 10 perturbed copies of each grasp: ±2 mm in position and ±3° in axis tilt, roughly the error of the pose estimate.
   - Robustness is the fraction of copies that still pass.
   - `quality = robustness × hold margin`.
8. **Resting-pose tags.** For each stable pose, rotate the grasp into "part resting on a plane". The grasp is usable in that pose if:
   - its approach axis is within 60° of straight down;
   - the head and the approach path stay above the support plane.

   This gives each grasp a list of usable resting poses.
9. **Prune.**
   - Non-maximum suppression: 5 mm and 10° apart, keeping the higher quality.
   - Keep at most 1000 grasps per part (step 3: raised from 300, because a top-300 crowds around the centre of mass), but at least 20 per resting pose wherever that many exist, so every pose is covered.
   - Write the database and a picture of the part with the top grasps drawn as cup squares, coloured by quality, one panel per resting pose.

Expected cost: a few minutes per part on the CPU, run under a 6 GB memory cap.

## Phase 2: grasp selection (execution time)

Module: `select_grasp.py`, function `select(part, T_robot_part, scene, q_now=None, k=5)`. A command-line wrapper reads JSON and writes JSON, and has a `--render` dry-run picture.

### Inputs

- part ID, which selects `db/<part>.npz` (loaded once and cached);
- `T_robot_part`: the part pose in `base_link`, as a 4×4 matrix;
- `scene`: the support-plane height and normal (conveyor or table), plus optional boxes such as the conveyor rails or neighbouring parts;
- `q_now`: the current joint state (optional), which seeds the IK and is used for the wrist-turn cost.

### Steps

1. **Transform.** `T_robot_grasp = T_robot_part · T_part_grasp`. There is no 90° expansion: the database already holds every collision-free turn.
2. **Resting pose.** Compare the part's up axis with the support normal to find which stable pose it is in. Keep only the grasps tagged for that pose. A pose that matches none (the part is tilted, leaning on something) falls back to step 3's direct checks on all grasps.
3. **Fast geometric filters.**
   - The approach axis must be within 60° of straight down.
   - The head at the grasp and at the pre-grasp (60 mm back) must stay clear of the support plane and the scene boxes. This uses the head's convex hulls against the planes and boxes, which takes milliseconds.
4. **Reachability.** Run the copied `ik.py` (G2 with the suck4 head, right arm). For each candidate, in order of quality:
   - an IK solution at the pre-grasp and at the grasp, with joints at least 5° inside their limits;
   - no self-collision and no collision with the scene.

   It stops after `k` reachable grasps or a 0.5 s budget.
5. **Guided local search (fallback).** Runs only if no database grasp survives. Around the best database grasps that failed only the reachability or scene checks:
   - re-sample the turn about the approach axis at any angle;
   - re-sample small offsets (±5 mm);
   - re-run the seal check of phase 1 online. The ray casts take milliseconds against the part mesh at the given pose.

   The database tells the search where to look; the online check makes sure each result is valid.
6. **Rank.** The score is `quality`, minus a small cost each for:
   - wrist turn from `q_now`;
   - distance to joint limits;
   - approach tilt.

   The function returns, best first:
   - tool pose and flange pose;
   - pre-grasp pose;
   - IK joint solution;
   - score parts;
   - database grasp ID.

Nothing in phase 2 moves the robot. On the real robot it only computes poses. Dry runs can be rendered as pictures.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`tests/`, synthetic meshes, seconds)

| Test | Expectation |
|---|---|
| Flat 200 mm plate | Grasps found at all turns. Every grasp near the centre passes, with robustness 1.0. |
| Plate with a 15 mm hole under one cup | 3 cups seal, so the grasp is valid. With the hole under 2 cups it is invalid. |
| Grasp over a plate edge | Rim rays miss, so the cup fails. |
| Cylinder of radius r | Seals for large r, fails once the rim deviation passes `SEAL_TOL` (the threshold matches the formula). |
| Deep 30 mm-wide channel | The cups fit but the head hits the walls, so the collision check rejects it. |
| Box | 3 pairs of stable faces. The resting-pose tags put each grasp on the faces that point up. |
| Frames | `T_robot_part · T_part_grasp` round trip. |
| Hold check | A heavy part with the grasp far from the centre of mass fails; at the centre of mass it passes. |

### Database checks (the 6 small parts)

- At least 20 grasps for every stable pose with probability > 5%, or a report of which poses have none.
- The grasp `build_parts.py` already found for the flat pose is close to (within 10 mm of) a database grasp, or is beaten by one.
- Visual review of `db/<part>_grasps.png`: no grasps should sit over holes or edges.
- Cross-check against the simulation's cup rule: at least 95% of the database grasps also pass `grasp_eval.SuctionEvaluator` on the true part.

### Selection checks (synthetic scenes, no robot)

- 200 random part poses: random stable pose, random position and turn on the conveyor area, using the sim's `conveyor.py` ranges. Record:
  - the share of scenes with at least one reachable grasp;
  - time per call (target < 0.5 s; median and 95th percentile);
  - how often the fallback search runs and how often it helps.
- Pose noise: add ±2 mm and ±2° to `T_robot_part` before selecting, then check the chosen grasp against the true pose with the seal rule. Report the drop in seal rate.

### Simulation validation (`validate_in_sim.py`, uses `g2_pybullet_sim`)

- **The same 40 evaluation scenes** (`EVAL_SEED` 90000) with the planned expert. The new top grasp replaces the current model grasp, and the whole task is run: reach, grab, carry home.
  - Compare with the current results: 85% (random base placement) and 98–100% (chosen placement).
  - Target: at least as good on flat scenes.
- **Other resting poses.** The simulation only places parts flat today, so `sample_scene` needs a stable-pose option. That is a small change in `g2_pybullet_sim`, to be agreed before making it.
  - Run 40 scenes in non-flat poses. There is no baseline, because the current model grasp only covers the flat pose.
- Pictures:
  - the database per part;
  - dry-run renders of the chosen posture for a few scenes;
  - a bar chart of new vs current success per part.

### Real robot (later, only with your go-ahead)

- Dry run only: take poses from the pose-estimation service, call `select`, and render the chosen IK posture over the camera image. Nothing is sent to the arm.

## Open items and assumptions

- **Part frame.** The database must use the same mesh and frame as the pose estimator. The JT → PLY conversion needs to be redone or taken from the pose-estimation models.
- **Gripper.** EVS08 with custom 24 mm cups, 50 mm apart, vacuum 75 kPa, safety 1.5 g (2026-10-22). Still placeholders:
  - bellows compliance (`seal_tol`) and travel (`cup_reach`);
  - the body's mounting angle on the flange (115 mm side along flange x);
  - the cups screwing straight into the body, with no stand-off;
  - the hose fitting on the side of the body, which is not modelled.
- **Seal rules differ.** The rim-ray rule is stricter than the simulation's single-ray rule. Both are reported, and the real cups decide which one fits.
- **Sheet thickness.** The CAD may have only one surface per sheet. If so, the "both sides" sampling uses the flipped normal, and the collision model needs the thickened patches from `build_parts.py`.
- **Tilted parts.** A part leaning on a rail or on another part matches no stable pose. Phase 2 handles this with direct checks, but there is no database tag for it.
- **Out of scope:**
  - motion planning to the grasp (only the reachability check);
  - the left arm;
  - learned scoring (GQ-CNN). The current learned suction scorer works on camera height maps, which this model-based pipeline does not use.

`select_grasp.py`: the `Selector(part).select(T_base_part, support, boxes, q_now, k=5, budget=2.0)` function, a JSON command-line wrapper (`examples/request_A5917204300.json`, which takes `T_base_cad_mm` directly) and a dry-run render (`Selector.render`).

- **Steps:**
  1. Transform the database grasps into the base frame.
  2. Find the resting pose: the support normal against each stable pose's down direction, within 15°.
  3. Fast filters:
     - approach at most 60° from straight down;
     - the EVS08 body box at least 2 mm above the support plane at the grasp and at the pre-grasp. The body box was added to `gripper.json` as `body_box`.
  4. IK:
     - pre-grasp 100 mm back (the plan said 60 mm; changed to match the 100 mm approach the database was checked with);
     - 4 approach points, then the grasp, each seeded from the previous solution;
     - joints at least 5° inside their limits;
     - obstacles: part, scene boxes and floor. Cups may touch the part only at the grasp.
  6. Rank by score = quality − 0.1 × tilt / 60° − 0.1 × wrist turn from `q_now` − 0.1 × limit closeness.
- **IK changes** (`ik.py`, the pipeline's copy):
  - a `seed` posture for `solve`;
  - an `ignore` set; the idle left arm is not checked against the scene.
  - Body joints (lift and waist) are active, as in the sim. Arm only, nothing on the conveyor is reachable.
- **Heading order:** most IK failures are the wrist's limits (joint 7 ±88°, joint 6 ±58°): most head turns can't be reached. Grasps whose tool x axis heads within ±75° of 120° (base frame) are tried first. Measured: 60–180° → 78/100 reachable, −90–0° → 1/75. Nothing is dropped.
- **Test scenes** (`tests/scenes.py`) copy the sim's conveyor:
  - belt top 0.74 m, 0.5 m wide, 40 mm rails;
  - robot 0.40–0.60 m from the near rail, ±0.30 m sideways, ±30° heading;
  - the part in a stable pose drawn by probability, random yaw and position between the rails.
  - Pose noise is ±2 mm per axis plus up to 2° about the part's centre. The CAD origin is 1–2 m from the part, so a turn about it would move the part about 50 mm.

### 2026-10-22: step 5 done (guided local search)

`Selector.local_search` runs only when the database pass finds no reachable grasp, with its own budget (default 3 s).

- **Seeds:** the 30 best database grasps of the resting pose, at least 10 mm apart.
- **Variants:** for each seed, its position plus 2 random shifts (±5 mm in the cup plane) × turns about the approach axis in 10° steps. Only turns whose tool x heading lies in the reachable wrist band are kept. About 1,350 variants.
- **Online checks** (part frame, exact mesh, loaded on first use):
  - seal rule;
  - fast filters (tilt, body box above the support);
  - head collision including the support plane;
  - hold margin;
  - seal robustness (10 perturbations).
- The first 60 variants that pass go to the same IK as step 4.
- **Code changes:**
  - `try_grasps` holds the IK and ranking loop shared by both passes;
  - `candidates_for` holds the fast filters on any base-frame poses;
  - results carry `source` (`database` or `local search`);
  - `select(..., use_database=False)` tests the search alone.
- **`tests/eval_select.py`** (same 204 scenes) now reports which pass produced the grasp. `--search-only` writes `logs/eval_select_search_only.json`.

### 2026-10-22: step 6 done (simulation validation)

`validate_in_sim.py` is the only file that uses `g2_pybullet_sim`.

- **Separate processes.** Both projects have an `ik.py`, so the 4 stages each run in their own process: `frames`, `scenes`, `select` (pipeline venv) and `run` (sim venv).
- **Sim swaps, at runtime only** (no sim file edited; the sim writes its URDF cache `build/evs08.urdf`):
  - the robot is replaced by this project's EVS08 URDF (registered as `evs08`; `suck4` requests are mapped to it);
  - parts use the sim's fine 600-piece collision model.
- **Frames.** The sim and CAD meshes come from the same conversion, with the same vertex order, so a rigid fit on the vertices gives CAD → sim exactly (0.0000 mm). The sim's flat pose is resting pose 1 for every part.
- **Scenes:** 10 flat and 10 other-pose scenes per part (120), random robot placement, the sim's conveyor.
  - Flat scenes use the sim's own `sample_scene`.
  - Other-pose scenes place the part between the rails like `tests/scenes.py`; the env's part pose is overridden.
- **Pick task:** the planned expert (`motion_plan.PlannedExpert`): reach, grab, carry home, with every attempt checked by simulating it. The pipeline's grasps are tried in order (top 5), and the first that plans wins. The baseline is the sim's model grasp with 4 turns.
- **Memory fixes:**
  - `Selector.close()` disconnects its PyBullet worlds, which garbage collection does not free;
